# Case Practice Index — Sector × Case Type × Difficulty

A single index over every practice case and drill in the skill, so a coach — or Claude running a self-study loop — can pick the *next* case deliberately instead of reading a bank front to back. Every row points at material that already exists elsewhere in `references/`; nothing here is a new case.

Three things this index is for:

1. **Pick by gap, not by order.** The coverage matrix shows which sector × case-type cells have never been practised.
2. **Ladder the difficulty.** Foundation → Core → Advanced is applied on one definition across every bank, so "one level up" means the same thing everywhere.
3. **Balance the five skills.** Structuring, math, synthesis, creativity, judgement. Most candidates over-practise structuring and under-practise judgement.

**Difficulty definitions**

| Level | What puts a case at this level |
|---|---|
| Foundation | One dominant lever, a standard tree, arithmetic that fits on a line. Builds the reflex. |
| Core | A full case: structure, a multi-step quantified spine, a trade-off, and a recommendation with risks. The interview default. |
| Advanced | At least one of — a false premise or trap in the prompt, multi-stage or reconciled math, an unconventional domain, or a judgement call the numbers do not settle. |

**Primary skill** is the dimension a case *most* tests, not the only one. Score every case on all five using the rubric in `references/case-facilitation-and-scoring.md`.

## How to use as a curriculum

A four-week ladder. Each week pairs a reading — `references/case-type-cheat-sheets.md` — with a scoring discipline from `references/case-facilitation-and-scoring.md`. Roughly four to six cases a week plus daily drills. Compress or stretch the calendar; keep the order.

| Week | Cases to draw | Cheat-sheet use | Scoring use | Daily drill |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Foundation** | Every **Foundation** row, any order | Read Cards 1–6 end to end once, then re-read the matching card *before* each case | Score **Structure** and **Communication** only. Do not chase the answer yet | Three Part A/B guesstimates from `references/guesstimate-drill-bank.md` |
| **2 — Core types** | Two **Core** rows per case type: profitability, market entry, new product, pricing, M&A, growth | Read the card cold *before* the case; afterwards check your tree against that card's clarifier bank and approach buckets | All five dimensions. Log every case and run the **case debrief card** after each | Three Part C/D guesstimates plus four Error Hunt items (`references/case-cracking-drills.md` Part D) |
| **3 — Advanced & unconventional** | **Advanced** rows, plus all of `references/case-bank-unconventional.md` (U1–U8 and the social-sector method) | Narrow to two things — the **4A customer-access lens** and the **recommendation guardrails** | All five, weighted to **Judgement** and **Synthesis**: the two that separate Core from Advanced | Part E decomposition and reconciliation drills (`references/case-cracking-drills.md`) |
| **4 — Mixed timed mocks** | Random draw across sectors and types, with no warning of the case type | None before the case. Use the cards only in the debrief, to find which card you *should* have reached for | Full rubric, 30–35 minutes, interviewer-led. Target a 3.0 average with no dimension below 2.5 | One cold guesstimate a day, timed to five minutes |

**Rules that make the ladder work**

- **Never run two cases in the same sector back to back.** The index exists to force variety; the matrix below shows where you are thin.
- **Promote a case only once.** A case you have seen tests recall, not structuring. Re-run it only to rebuild a weak dimension.
- **Week 2 is the long one.** If the schedule slips, stretch week 2 and protect week 4 — timed mixed mocks are what the interview actually is.
- **Stop and repair.** Two consecutive cases below 2 on the same dimension mean a drill week, not another case.

## How Claude should use this file

- **When asked for "a case to practise"**, read this index first, ask for the target difficulty and any sector to avoid, then name one row and open only that file. Do not load a whole bank.
- **When asked to build a study plan**, use the four-week ladder above and fill it from the master table, honouring the no-repeat-sector rule.
- **When asked what is weak**, compare the cases already run against the coverage matrix and name the empty cells, not a generic list of case types.
- **Never attribute a case to an outside source.** These are the skill's own worked cases; present them as such, and see the *Source Discretion & Quantification Standard* in `SKILL.md`.
